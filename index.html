Wearable Voice-Recorder Enclosure

3D-printed two-part case for a 6-layer wearable recorder PCB, taken from CAD through printed prototypes across two board generations.

The fit problem: First prints didn't seat. I measured the cavity, found the clearance was under-allocated for FDM shrinkage, and re-budgeted it from 0.4 to 1.2 mm per side. The parts now close.

Details
  • Reverse-engineered component placement from Gerber, Excellon and pick-and-place files
  • Designed for manufacturability: parting line oriented for layer-adhesion strength, draft, uniform 1.5 mm walls
  • Integrated an FR-4 board, NdFeB magnet, Li battery and USB-C port
  • Class-A surfacing for a premium finish

Board 30.18 × 40.39 × 1.3 mm, 6 layers · Case ~35 × 46 × 19.5 mm · Wall 1.5 mm

Formula SAE Harness Anchorage (Composite Chassis)

Designed and hand-analyzed the lower harness mounts (machined spreader, bonded insert and eyebolt) for a carbon monocoque, from FSAE rules to a release-ready model.

Key finding: A −0.20 margin that looked like a failed design was the wrong limit. Pin-in-hole loads need MMPDS bearing values, not tensile ones. Rechecked, the margin was positive.

  • Zero prying: put the load line on the bolt axis, removing 450 N·m from the joint
  • ~1.2 kg saved by finding the real mass drivers. The insert alone went from 76 g to 29 g.
  • Rules drove geometry: edge distance, not strength, set the Ø55 size
Details
  • Checked net tension, bearing and shear-out on every load path using MMPDS bearing allowables (Fbru ≈ 1.8 Ftu)
  • Designed bolted joints for bearing, not friction (preload slip ~10 kN vs 33 kN required)
  • Chose 7050-T7451 for corrosion resistance and syntactic foam core, since honeycomb (<1 MPa) couldn't take 6.72 MPa
  • Isolated carbon from aluminium to prevent galvanic corrosion
  • Aligned the service load with the certified test direction (F.7.9.2.b)

VEX U Robotics: Elevator Lift

On our VEX U team, every mechanism goes from an Onshape model to a physical prototype to code. We design in CAD, build from the model, test, and feed what we learn back into the design.

Example, the elevator: a multi-stage, string-driven lift powered by two V5 motors. I modeled the stages, pulleys and motor mount in Onshape to check fit, clearances and string routing before building, then built and tested the prototype.

  • CAD first: caught fit and clearance problems in Onshape instead of in metal
  • Prototype and iterate: built from the model, tested it, and updated the CAD with the fixes
  • Programming: wrote the robot code in VEX's VS Code extension to control the lift and other mechanisms
Details
  • Pulley rigging: routing string so all stages extend together, and managing tension and slack
  • Designing around a fixed parts kit: C-channel, shafts, bearings and motor mounts
  • Shared Onshape models so teammates could design connecting mechanisms in parallel
